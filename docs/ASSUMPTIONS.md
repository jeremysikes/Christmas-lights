# LightPlan Phase 1 and 1.1 — Assumptions

These were fixed before building. Each one is a place where a real house or a real product can differ from the model, so each is editable or flagged in the UI.

1. **Units and electrical context.** US feet and inches, 120 V North American residential circuits, USD. Product specs (watts, max strings end to end, prices) are typical retail values, not one brand. All of it lives in an editable JSON product library.
2. **Camera model.** Pinhole camera, square pixels, principal point at the image center, no lens-distortion correction. Phone main cameras correct distortion in software; 0.5x ultra-wide photos will measure less accurately.
3. **Focal length.** Taken from the photo's EXIF data when present (ignored when the EXIF image size shows the photo was cropped). Otherwise estimated from the perspective rectangle when the photo is angled enough. Otherwise assumed to be a phone main camera (26 mm equivalent), which caps roof and ground items at Low. Whatever the source, it is checked against the rectangles in the photo, and a mismatch is flagged with the measured value. Screenshots carry no EXIF and may be zoomed, so keep "Automatic" once a rectangle is marked; the "Screenshot (wide view)" preset (90° field of view) only fits an unzoomed wide view.
4. **Geometry.** Walls are vertical planes and eaves are horizontal. A gable rake seen face-on lies in the wall plane, so it measures at true length and is used to estimate roof pitch. Roof slopes and the ground (walkways, driveways) are derived planes, computed from the wall's recovered camera pose, so receding rakes, ridges and walkway edges get real 3D lengths; they are very sensitive to tap error, which their confidence reflects. Trees and bushes traced on a wall stand in front of it, so their sizes are approximate and flagged; traced on a ground plane they are measured on a vertical plane through their base.
5. **Waste.** 10% is added to every linear run for waste, corners and drops to the outlet (editable). Strings are counted along each chain of connected runs (a string carries on around a corner), and only the strings that cover the true length are hung and counted as load; the rest are spares. Cut-to-length wire is the same by the foot: the feet with waste are bought, the true length is hung. Bush and tree counts use rules of thumb instead of a waste factor.
6. **Power.** Continuous load on a circuit is kept at or below 80% of the breaker rating (15 A = 1,440 W, 20 A = 1,920 W). Everything plugged into one outlet shares one receptacle, one timer and a chain of power stakes, all 15 A plug-in gear, so an outlet also carries at most 1,440 W whatever the breaker. Cords are sized on the standard lengths actually bought, by ampacity (capped at the 15 A plug) and a 3% voltage-drop limit. One timer or smart plug per outlet used; more than 1,250 W of incandescent load through one timer is flagged, and an outlet that carries more than the chosen supplier's timer is rated for (1,000 W at 1000Bulbs) gets a warning and a heavy-duty timer on the shopping list instead. Extra cords at an outlet use power stakes (ground stakes with 3 outlets) chained from the timer's socket. Cord distances between walls, roof and ground are worked out in 3D from the photo.
7. **Storage and files.** Autosave stays in this browser only (localStorage for the project, IndexedDB for the photo, every access wrapped in try/catch). When storage is blocked, a note says the plan will be lost on close and offers Save project; an autosave this version cannot read is kept as a backup to download; when another tab saves over the plan, autosave pauses until the user picks one. Photos are downscaled to 2048 px on the long side; the measurement math does not depend on resolution. Inside the claude.ai artifact viewer, exports go through the viewer's download prompt; as a standalone file, they are normal browser downloads. The PDF is generated in the browser because the artifact frame blocks the print dialog.
8. **Camera capture.** Uses the phone's own camera app through the file picker (`capture` attribute), because the artifact frame blocks live camera APIs.
9. **Cords run along the ground (Phase 1.1).** A cord from an outlet goes down to the ground, along the ground and straight up to where it plugs in; it is never laid across a wall face. A cord never passes through a traced window or door: it goes up half a foot beside the opening (or in the gap between two openings that nearly touch, or over a row of windows that touch) and across at the height of the lights. The outlet's own cord drops straight down from the outlet. Outlets are taken to be 1.5 ft above the ground unless the photo says otherwise. The length bought is the route × 1.15 plus slack (4 ft for a cord from an outlet, 1.5 ft for a jumper), rounded up to a standard cord length (6, 15, 25, 50, 100 ft). A cord that runs in front of a traced door, or lies across more than 1.5 ft of a walkway where it meets the wall, gets a cord cover and a warning; a cord that stops at the door jamb just inside the walkway edge does not.
10. **Runs are linked with jumper cords (Phase 1.1).** Runs that sit close together are joined with short jumper cords (at most 30 ft; a straight hop only within 6 ft), so only a few cords go back to the outlets. A jumper plugs into the end of the run before it (then its load passes through that run, which must stay within what the product allows end to end) or into a 3-way tap where that run's own cord arrives. Every cord back to the outlet carries everything hanging off it and must stay within the 15 A plug limit; the outlet and its circuit keep their limits. Voltage drop is added up along the cords of a path (3% limit). The switch "Link nearby runs with jumper cords" turns this off; "Give this run its own cord" keeps one run on its own cord from the outlet (it can still be fed from its middle, with both halves on one tap).
11. **Supplier prices are dated snapshots (Phase 1.1).** The supplier catalog (Christmas Lights Etc, 1000Bulbs) is a hand-entered list of part numbers, prices and links that were checked against each supplier's own pages on the date shown ("as of Nov 11, 2026"). An item read on its own product page says "checked"; an item seen on the supplier's category list only, a bulb colour with no item on file, and a product picked in a colour it is not sold in say "check the price". Nothing is fetched: LightPlan never contacts a supplier, so a price can be out of date. Wholesale (account) prices are not public; type your own price over any line. Watts and connect limits come from the supplier's listing; where a page gives two figures the lower one is used.
12. **Cut-to-length C7/C9 (Phase 1.1).** Socket wire on one cord is limited to 250 ft of wire or 250 bulbs, whichever comes first (the trade rule for 18-gauge SPT-1 wire), whatever larger figure a page prints. The feet with waste are bought; the true length is cut, hung and lit, and the rest stays on the spool. Wire is bought as the mix of sizes that costs least once every piece is counted as $5 of plugs and work, a listed-only price as 10% dearer, and no more than four ready-made lengths per product. Slide-on plugs are counted for the cut ends and joints that have no plug of their own. A bulb colour sold as a hungrier bulb line counts at its own watts.
13. **Bushes (Phase 1.1).** 100 mini lights for every 1.5 ft of height and every 3 ft of width (a bush 4.5 ft wide and 3 ft tall gets about 300 lights). Nets cover width × height × 1.5 to drape over the top and down the sides.
14. **Taps made zoomed out (Phase 1.1).** Every tapped point records how precisely it could be tapped at the zoom it was placed with. A point tapped at fit zoom on a phone can be off by 15 photo pixels or more, so the rating drops and the reason says to zoom in and re-tap.
15. **Out of scope for Phase 1.** Accounts, live pricing, supplier carts or feeds, Street View or aerial imagery (Google's terms do not allow tracing from Street View), automatic edge detection. See `PHASE2_ROADMAP.md`.
16. **Not an inspection.** The power plan is a planning estimate, not an electrical inspection.
